The Complete Video Production Process

A smooth video project is built in stages. Clear decisions before filming make production days more focused and give editors the material they need to shape a useful final story.

A shared brief helps everyone make consistent decisions from outline to final export. Each stage should reduce uncertainty for the next.

1. Define the brief

Name the audience, goal, message, action, channels, and success measures. Confirm scope, schedule, budget, and approver.

2. Prepare before the camera rolls

Plan the concept, script, shot list, locations, schedule, releases, and crew. Confirm logistics and formats early.

3. Film the planned story

Guide participants through interviews and action shots. Monitor sound and picture, and capture context for the edit.

4. Edit and refine

Shape pacing, sound, color, graphics, and music. Gather organized feedback against the brief from one decision-maker.

5. Approve and deliver

Check accuracy, captions, rights, and file types. See corporate video formats and the business video guide.

GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

What are the main stages of video production?

The usual stages are discovery and planning, pre-production, filming, editing and review, final approval, and delivery.

When should the script be approved?

Approve the script or interview outline before filming. That gives speakers and crew a clear direction and helps prevent avoidable reshoots.

How many edit rounds should a project include?

Agree on revision rounds in the project scope. The right number depends on complexity and how well the brief, script, and feedback process are defined.
